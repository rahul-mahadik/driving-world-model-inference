# Verifier research design: lossy video inference

**Status: prospective, unrun, not an executable worker specification.** This design asks whether a protected evaluator can distinguish acceptable changes to a generated driving video from real degradation while rewarding measured speed. It does not establish that the current verifier does so. The accompanying [gate register](../configs/research/verifier-gates-v2.json) has no fitted thresholds, no validation results and no enabled final reward. Historical quality failures and `qualified: false` remain unchanged.

The current configuration-only controller is described in [agent-environment.md](agent-environment.md). Its numerical/perceptual proxy checks are useful engineering contracts; this proposal requires additional validation before calling their output a quality label. The [code-action threat model](code-action-threat-model.md) addresses a separate, not yet implemented, arbitrary-code boundary.

## 1. Separate three questions

| Experiment | What it estimates | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same scene, seed, reference settings and independent repeat | Numerical repeatability, timing noise and measurement reliability | Acceptable semantic change |
| Same scene/seed, different precision | Drift caused by that precision choice on that runtime | That the precision change is harmless |
| Same scene/settings, different reference seed | Variability among reference futures | A universal tolerance for any candidate output |

A seed change can produce an equally plausible but visually different future. Conversely, a candidate can stay close in pixels while losing a pedestrian or ignoring an action. The empirical reference distributions replace an unexplained constant such as `0.01` as **calibration evidence**, not as an automatic quality decision. Precision changes need the same independent acceptance checks as other candidates.

Use seed-matched LPIPS, RGB error and temporal-change diagnostics for cheap development feedback. Exclude conditioning frames from future-quality scores; preserve their separate identity checks. Compute whole-future diagnostics so a candidate cannot corrupt frames outside a known scoring sample. These metrics are observations, not a claim of driving safety or perceptual equivalence.

## 2. Freeze the labels, partitions and decision rule

Partition by source drive/scene group before generating data: metric development, threshold calibration, locked verifier audit, optimizer development and final candidate evaluation. Neighboring clips and all seeds/repeats/derived corruptions of a scene stay in one partition. A scene manifest must document existing public exposure; evaluator-held inputs are not necessarily secret from model pretraining. Never retune a gate on its locked audit or final candidate outputs. A failed audit requires a new policy version and new audit data.

The bounded first pilot uses **16 calibration scene groups and 16 distinct audit scene groups**, with two reference seeds and two independent repeats per seed: 128 reference requests. It adds a development-only panel of four prospectively specified perturbation families on eight calibration scenes: 32 candidate requests, using a fixed seed and one repeat. This is 160 measured generations before warmups, metrics and any support proofs. Exact scene IDs, seeds, implementations, order and time/storage caps must be registered before admission. The four families are a precision change, a reduced-step setting, an aggressive-cache setting and a separately verified implementation-preserving reference control. If an implementation is unavailable, preserve that family as unavailable; do not silently replace it. The pilot is an integration and error-discovery study, not a calibrated-quality or optimizer-comparison result.

For semantic labels, use two independent blinded human reviewers and adjudicate disagreement with a third reviewer, preserving the original decisions. Predeclare a rubric covering visible scene geometry/objects, temporal coherence, conditioning consistency and action adherence; allow an uncertain label. Reviewer identities can be pseudonymous, but do not count an automatic metric or an LLM judge as independent human review. Keep uncertain cases in the report and out of denominators that require a known label. Separately report their prevalence and whether the verifier accepts them.

Known-good controls include independently accepted reference seed changes and exact replay through the trusted identity path. Known-bad controls include explicitly labeled failures of the rubric. Frame freezing, temporal shuffling, wrong action/conditioning, missing objects, blur and selected-frame corruption probe specific failure modes. A “few steps” or “aggressive cache” configuration is only a **candidate negative control**: its output must actually fail the rubric before it is labeled bad. Do not define the labels using the same metric being evaluated. Derived controls must retain their original array hashes, transformation specification and transformed-output hashes.

Calibrate limits only on the calibration partition. Choose a small predeclared metric family and record the selected threshold, uncertainty procedure, feature/checkpoint identities and every tried alternative. Optimize acceptance of labeled-good examples subject to the predeclared false-accept constraint. If the positive and negative distributions overlap too strongly, the verifier must abstain or fail validation; increasing a tolerance after seeing the audit is prohibited.

## 3. Two-level final assessment

**Per-output checks:** shape/range/finiteness, full-frame coverage, conditioning/action binding, fresh execution provenance, a validated trajectory diagnostic, and any development-validated artifact detectors. Missing evidence or an unvalidated required metric gives `inconclusive`. A verified violation gives `fail`.

**Distribution-level check:** compare candidate and reference future distributions on the same held-out scene/action mixture. This is a batch statistic, not a score for one request. Standard FVD can be insensitive to temporal corruption; its feature choice can reward good-looking static frames. That limitation is documented in [Ge et al., CVPR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12391). Finite-sample Gaussian-feature distance estimates can also change rankings through estimation bias, as shown for FID by [Chong and Forsyth, CVPR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07023). Applying that warning to small-sample video estimates is a methodological precaution, not a claim that the image paper validated this task.

The first alternative to investigate is a pinned video encoder with a prespecified MMD estimator. [JEDi](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05203v2) combines V-JEPA features and a polynomial-kernel MMD; its [author repository](https://github.com/oooolga/JEDi) is available. Its reported performance elsewhere does not validate it for these driving clips. Freeze preprocessing, temporal sampling, kernel and normalization; test repeatability, temporal corruption sensitivity and action/scene-stratum behavior locally. If using an unbiased MMD-squared estimator, retain negative finite-sample estimates rather than silently clipping them in statistical analysis.

Use a separate calibration sample-size curve, scene-cluster resampling and known perturbations to estimate precision, ranking stability and detection power. An ordinary non-significant difference is **not** evidence of equivalence. A pass requires an upper confidence bound on a predeclared degradation statistic to fall within a calibrated noninferiority margin, plus all mandatory per-output checks. The estimator, margin, bootstrap/permutation procedure and sample count must be frozen before audit. Reference-versus-reference draws must preserve independent generation seeds and the matching scene structure. If available data do not support a stable bound, report the distribution check as inconclusive. Sixteen clips, or 64 correlated clips from 16 scenes, cannot be relabeled as a validated population-level quality test.

## 4. Validate trajectory measurement before making it a gate

Vista's final [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.17398v5), section 4.2, trains an inverse-dynamics model and measures trajectory error over two seconds; it also reports error on real videos. Its [official code repository](https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/Vista) is verified; a usable public inverse-dynamics checkpoint/evaluation bundle was **not verified** in this review. The upstream [IDM request](https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/Vista/issues/55) is not evidence of an available evaluator. Do not invent a checkpoint or treat a paper description as an implemented metric in this benchmark.

Before adoption, pin an obtainable, appropriately licensed estimator and evaluate it on held-out real driving clips with independently recorded ego poses/actions. Register camera coordinates, scale, time interval, frame rate and valid horizons; report trajectory ADE/FDE and heading error with units and confidence intervals. Check straight driving, turns, stops, moving foreground objects, low texture and blur. For monocular odometry, explicitly handle scale ambiguity; per-candidate alignment must not erase action errors. Prespecify exclusion rules and report failures as missing/inconclusive rather than zero error.

Then test calibrated transforms and wrong-action controls, and blind-review estimator failures on generated clips. Passing on real videos alone does not establish accuracy on generated artifacts. Report the estimator's own error floor, uncertainty and dependence on generator training data. Until this validation succeeds, trajectory estimates remain diagnostics and the requested combined distribution/trajectory gate remains disabled.

## 5. Audit false acceptance, false rejection and abstention

Report the full label-by-decision table for `pass`, `fail`, `inconclusive` and execution failure. False acceptance is accepted labeled-bad cases divided by all labeled-bad cases. Report both strict false rejection of labeled-good cases and conservative good-case nonacceptance, where `fail` and `inconclusive` both count. This prevents an always-abstaining gate from looking successful. Separate infrastructure failures from quality decisions and report them without dropping rows.

The pilot reports observed rates and uncertainty, including bad-control families separately, without claiming a 5% bound. For zero errors in 16 independent scene cases, the one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is about **17.1%**. For one prespecified endpoint, 59 zero-error independent cases are needed for an upper bound below 5%. These are optimistic independence examples; many seeds/frames/controls from one scene do not multiply the number of independent scenes.

The later validation target is simultaneously bounding bad-case acceptance and good-case nonacceptance by 5%, using one-sided exact bounds with Bonferroni allocation of a total 5% error probability. This needs at least **72 zero-error independent cases per endpoint**; plan 80 audit scene groups as an initial target, not a guarantee of success. Each scene contributes a predeclared conservative summary per class (any bad acceptance; any good nonacceptance), so clustered variants cannot inflate sample size. Audit scene sampling and label composition must match the population to which the claim applies. If errors occur, the bound may exceed the target even with 80 scenes. Family-specific claims need their own power and multiplicity plan. No small mixed-control audit establishes a guarantee against all attacks.

Exit artifacts are the frozen policy/configuration, split and label manifests, metric/source/checkpoint hashes, raw evidence inventory, sample-size/power analysis, full confusion tables, confidence bounds, abstention coverage, failure ledger and independent review. **All are currently unrun or unfilled.** Only after these gates pass may a future study emit a limited statement such as “accepted by verifier version X on the registered test distribution.” It must not claim universal quality preservation or safe closed-loop driving.

## 6. Cost and release boundary

The 160-generation pilot is a proposed count, not resource admission. Historical 45–60 second full-model requests imply about 2–2.7 GPU-hours of measured generation alone; compilation, warmups, metric inference, failures, retained raw arrays, transfer, independent verification and cleanup are additional. Existing storage and prepaid balances require a fresh audit. Stop after the pilot if the larger verifier or table cannot fit the remaining project cap; publish the measured limitation. No old timing result receives a new quality label, and no failed or partial attempt is erased.
